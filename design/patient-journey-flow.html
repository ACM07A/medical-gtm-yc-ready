The handoff — who owns what

the facilitator/provider line, made explicit

MedYatra owns (facilitator)

  • Lead, all comms & nurture, qualification
  • Relaying the requirement & reports to the hospital
  • Supporting documents — the hospital invitation letter + a visa checklist
  • Light coordination; payment facilitation & fee transparency
  • Chasing the hospital; keeping the patient warm

Hospital owns (provider)

  • Medical opinion, diagnosis, treatment recommendation
  • The quote / package price & what it includes
  • Every clinical follow-up question
  • Appointment slot, admission date, surgeon assignment
  • Visa-invitation letter, pre-op instructions, the procedure
  • Discharge summary, tele-follow-up, clinical outcomes

Patient owns: their own flights/tickets + their own visa application (using our supporting docs). Partners / operator (tenant) own: interpreter, airport transfers & accommodation. MedYatra keeps the coordination layer deliberately light.

Rule: anything clinical (diagnosis, suitability, medication, risk) is ⇄ handed to the hospital's medical team — MedYatra never advises. This is the facilitator boundary and the liability firewall.

Rules at every step

Clinical → hospital, always
Any diagnosis/suitability/medication/risk question routes to the hospital's doctors. MedYatra never advises. The liability firewall.
Material change → re-consent
If on-arrival findings change plan or price, stop and re-quote + re-consent before proceeding.
Human gate
No template, quote, or clinical message auto-sends. Approved in the Studio.
24h session timer
Each inbound resets it; outside the window you must use an approved template.
Opt-out = instant
STOP honoured immediately, suppressed on all channels.
Escalate to human agent
Complex medical Q, strong objection, high value, or "talk to a person."
Outcome logging
Every terminal state (won/lost/dormant) logs a reason → fit-model calibration.
Re-engagement cap
Dormant leads get a quarterly value touch only, unless opted out.
